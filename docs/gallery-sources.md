# Paddock gallery: image source candidates

Research date: 2026-10-09. Brief: premium motorsport photos (GT / endurance / sports cars on circuit) at sunset, golden hour, dusk or night with headlights, landscape, at least 2560 px wide (ideally 3840 or more), for 2560x1440 side screens.

40 candidates: top 25 first, then 15 extras. Nothing was downloaded. Every page below was fetched and the licence statement read on the page.

## How these were checked (read this first)

- No images were downloaded or looked at. "Light" is judged from the photographer's caption, Unsplash tags, the Wikimedia EXIF time stamp and (for Commons) the event. A quick contact-sheet look when thumbnails are first fetched is still worth doing.
- Pixel sizes are the original sizes. Unsplash: from Unsplash's own photo data (the page itself does not print pixels). Pexels: printed on the photo page. Wikimedia: printed on the file page.
- File size is only shown by Wikimedia. Unsplash and Pexels pages do not show it, so those cells say n/s.
- Aspect ratio is in brackets. 1.78 is exactly 16:9. 1.50 (3:2) loses about 16% of the height when cropped to 16:9. 1.33 (4:3) loses 25%. 2.00 needs a side crop.
- Unsplash+ items (plus.unsplash.com) were skipped. They are paid, not under the free Unsplash licence.

Other caveats:
- All Wikimedia items in this list are Flickr uploads. The pages I fetched carry the "Flickr images reviewed by FlickreviewR" marker, which confirms the licence was checked at upload.
- Race cars carry sponsor liveries (for example Coca-Cola, Konica Minolta, Red Bull). That is part of the cars, not watermarks, but it is "logo content" if you are strict.
- Wikimedia asks automated clients to send a descriptive User-Agent when fetching files.
- Pexels items by Tom Verdoot (#39, #40) have `.png` originals, so expect large files.
- Light on the Raphael Rousseau Le Mans shots (#19, #20, #27, #28) comes from Unsplash's captions ("at night with headlights"). The exposure data (1/125 s, ISO 6400) suggests dusk or floodlit light rather than full dark.

## Considered and left out

- Unsplash+ (plus.unsplash.com) results: paid, not covered by the free licence.
- Pexels photos 28944062, 15447427 and 31266786: sunset or dusk race cars but portrait or near-square.
- Pexels photo 29707621 (sports car at Zandvoort, sunset, listed as 7680x4800): the page lists "Topaz Photo AI" as the software, so it is probably an AI-upscaled smaller image. Pexels 37985719 (Nürburgring) was also processed with Topaz Photo.
- Pexels night-race series by user 2149489342 (for example 31032661): no camera data and no named venue, so it could not be shown to be non-AI.
- Pexels "Astesmedia" sunset race-car series (for example 35334771): road and coastal-road scenes, several parked.
- Unsplash night-only series with no dusk cue (Alexander Ediker at Daytona, Konara Bandara, Thibault Lam Tran, Raphael Rousseau's other night shots): usable as extras if you want more night material.
- Wikimedia items taken after dark or in the pits (for example Osajus "Magnus Ferrari" at 22:33, "Corvette C7,r" at 21:19, the Nic Redhead "at night" Le Mans set): moody but not golden hour, so not listed.
- Raw sizes: no Unsplash or Pexels candidate under 2560 px was kept. Only #31 is under 3840 px.

Nothing here is downloaded. 20 candidates were found and looked at as 960 px thumbnails; with Peter's no-heavy-blur rule applied, the best 12 are in the contact sheet `docs\gallery-preview-2.png` (page `docs\gallery-preview-2.html`, made with `tools\snap-gallery-preview-2.cmd`; Wikimedia thumbnails use the standard 960 px size).

- Licences, all free and all read on the file page or from the Commons API: CC BY 2.0 or CC BY-SA 2.0 for the 11 Wikimedia files (the Flickr licence review passed on each), the Pexels License for R20. No ND, no NC, nothing from Unsplash (its results were NASCAR, Formula 1 or paid Unsplash+). CC BY needs the photographer and licence named: the gallery caption does that ("... · Photo: Martin Lee · CC BY-SA 2.0").
- Sizes: every original is at least 2700 px wide (R20, the smallest, is 2700x1800; the rest 3997 px or more), so every one fills 2560x1440 after a 16:9 crop. Sizes in MB are Wikimedia's; Pexels does not show one. R17 (3.4 MB for 23 MP) is heavily compressed: check it at full size after download.
- The celebrations are the 24 Hours of Le Mans (WEC, Ferrari's 100th-anniversary win, 2023): free-licence IMSA podium, pit-wall, fist-out-of-the-window or fireworks pictures do not exist (searched Commons and Pexels); the one IMSA-weekend victory-lane moment (R8) has a watermark and a grey sky and was dropped.
- Downloading: Wikimedia answered the full-size originals with HTTP 429 in round 1, so when Peter says yes the plan is the same: fetch each file's standard 3840 px size (`.../thumb/<a>/<ab>/<File>/3840px-<File>`, one at a time with `curl.exe` and a descriptive User-Agent, pauses between files), which is still wider than the 2560 px needed; Pexels as the original link above.
- Picture dates: the EXIF clocks of several photographers are wrong (Osajus, United Autosports); dusk or night was judged from the picture itself and the event date is given.

- Light and subject (my own tag per picture): 51 cars on track at sunset, dusk or night (or sharp panning in good light), 24 IMSA/WEC multiclass (GTP, LMP2, GT3/GTE in one frame or hypercars), 17 celebrations (Le Mans podiums 2004 and 2023 incl. champagne and confetti, Ferrari's winner's lap with the flag, Daytona/Road Atlanta finish line, Super GT/F2/World RX champagne, IndyCar flag), 13 scenic or pit-lane moods (Daytona banking at twilight, Sebring sunset pit lane, Spa forest, Le Mans Classic pit straight).

Authors with the most pictures: Osajus Photography 26, Martin Lee 14, Mickael Le rouzic 9, MarcelX42 8, United Autosports 7.
- No more than 6 pictures from one event. The 11 round-2 seed pictures from the section above are all included (R1, R2, R4, R5, R9, R10, R11, R12, R16, R17, R18, R20).
- Looked at: about 8000 Commons files were harvested (CC BY / CC BY-SA / CC0 / PD, at least 2560 px wide, landscape), 682 thumbnails and 105 final crops were looked at by eye. About 330 thumbnails were rejected, among them about 25 for heavy motion blur (whole frame smeared, or a sharp car on a heavily streaked background; examples: the Nürburgring pans #1-#4 and many Road Atlanta night streaks) and the rest as static paddock/garage shots, portraits, crowded victory lanes or cars too small in the frame. 12 pictures were dropped after the final crop because the car was too small, too dark or the background was smeared.
- Downloaded: about 231 MB of 3840 px (Wikimedia) or 2560 px (Unsplash, Pexels) files for 118 candidates plus 31 MB of 500 px thumbnails, about 260 MB in all; kept 70 MB. Raw copies were deleted from the temp folder. Near-duplicates (average hash distance below 8) were checked against each other and the existing gallery; three similar-layout pairs were looked at and kept as different pictures.
- Sources that gave trouble: Wikimedia answers more than about 5 thumbnails a minute with HTTP 429 (and then a "robot policy" 429), and no longer serves a 2560 px thumbnail (HTTP 400, only the listed sizes such as 3840), so everything was fetched one file at a time with 11 s gaps. Unsplash's search API is closed (401), so Unsplash was read through its search pages and the `/download` redirect. Flickr was not used. No file was an archive or executable; every download was checked for JPEG/PNG magic bytes and loaded with System.Drawing.
