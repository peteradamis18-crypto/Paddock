# The Vault

Paddock keeps your best laps and races for you. You do not set anything up, and you do not press anything for a lap to be saved.

## What it keeps

After a session it reads the telemetry and keeps:

- a new personal best for that car and track
- a lap in the fastest 10% of your laps in that car at that track
- your three quickest laps in that car at that track
- a race you won, or finished in the top 3
- a race where your iRating went up by 40 or more

Those are keepers. They are never deleted to make space. Each one has its own copy of the telemetry file, under `vault\telemetry`, so it is still there if the original is cleaned up. The copy is the original file, untouched.

Other recent laps stay for 30 days, then drop off. A lap you star yourself is a keeper too.

## Where you open it

On the top screen: Sessions, then **Vault ›**. After a new personal best, the debrief has **In the Vault**.

The page is a grid of cards, grouped by day (the day header stays at the top while you scroll) and then by session. A lap that was recorded shows a real picture from its video (irdash cuts one frame with ffmpeg in the background, never while you drive, into `vault\thumbs\f-<id>.jpg`; `GET /vault/frame?id=`), with the video's length bottom-right; a lap without video shows the track map with its line. Each card has the lap time big, how far it is from your best, and the result as a coloured word: purple FASTEST EVER, green NEW PB (it was a personal best that day), gold WIN / PODIUM / TOP 3 EVER. `/vault/list` gives each moment `vid` (its recorder clip), `vidDur` and `frame`. Views: All, Keepers, Races, Best laps (grouped by car and track, quickest first), Videos; Car / Track (and Tag, Year when there is more than one) open big lists; S / M / L card sizes are remembered. Arrows move between cards, Enter opens one.

A recorded lap opens on the Vault's own player: the video big, PLAY, -5 s / +5 s, SOUND ON / MUTED and a volume slider (remembered), EXPAND (the video fills the page; E key, Escape makes it smaller) and FULL SCREEN (F key), the SYNC nudge, and under it your speed, throttle and brake and the time difference against another lap, all on one cursor. A lap without video opens the analysis workbench; a race with a race log opens its RACE page. BACK goes back to the cards. The corner table shows each turn against your personal best and against the best lap of that turn in the Vault. You can write a note.

**Open in Cosworth** opens the lap in Cosworth Pi Toolbox (with Pi's iRacing workbook, the lap added to it). **Share** opens the folder. **Delete** asks twice.

Compare two laps of the same car and track from the list on the left. The difference trace shows where one is faster.

## Space

It uses up to 60 GB on this PC. The page shows how much is used. Keepers are never removed to stay inside that. Only old clips and recent laps that were not kept are.

It stays on this PC. It is not in OneDrive. It does not change any iRacing file. It only reads telemetry, and it waits while you are driving. A file that is still online-only in OneDrive is skipped, so it does not start a download. While a session is running it leaves the newest telemetry file alone. Once the session is over (you left it, or the race has its flag and you are out of the car) and that file has stopped growing, it reads it even though the sim program is still open, so the after-race laps appear straight away.

## Video

Paddock has its own video recorder. It is **off** until the lead has measured what it costs in the sim (`recorder=0` in `dash\irdash.ini`, the shipped setting).

When it is on you do nothing. While you are in the car Paddock films the middle screen (small hidden program, no window, never takes the focus) and keeps the last three minutes. When a lap ends it keeps a clip of that lap if it is your best clean lap of the session so far, a personal best, or you pressed KEEP. The clip starts a second or two before the lap and ends a second after it. It goes in the Vault next to that lap. It stops when you leave the car or the sim closes.

- A lap in the pits, off the track for more than 0.75 s, or after a tow or reset is not clean and gets no clip (unless you pressed KEEP).
- Clips of personal bests and KEEP clips are never deleted to make space. The others go after the Vault's "recent" days (30), or sooner when the Vault is over its space.
- It does not keep clips when less than 20 GB is free on the disk. `dash\irdash.log` says so.
- If the screen comes out black (iRacing in full-screen exclusive mode cannot be recorded) the log says "the recording is BLACK ... choose Windowed or Borderless in iRacing". The Rig Doctor can read the same words from `/vault/recorder` (`note`).
- If the recorder program stops by itself it is started again, at most 3 times in a session, each time logged. After that it gives up until the next session and says so.

It uses the NVIDIA video chip (HEVC `hevc_nvenc` at about 8 Mbit/s), not the processor, so the game should feel nothing. If the chip refuses, it tries h264_nvenc, then the processor (libx264). Every HEVC file carries `hvc1` and `faststart` so iPhones and Safari play it. That is a claim to check: the lead measures the frame rate in a full race with `recorder=1` before it stays on (steps in `docs\tasks\log\C-06.md`).

**Sound** (2026-10-18, `dash\src\recaudio.cs`, `docs\design\audio-recording.md`). The videos carry the game's sound: only iRacing and CrewChief (Windows "process loopback"), so music or Discord never get into a clip; only if Windows cannot pick them out is the whole PC recorded. It runs exactly while the picture does, writes `vault\rec\aud-<start µs>.pcm` chunks on the same wall clock, and every session file and clip gets its sound encoded once (AAC 160 kbit/s) while the picture is still copied. Sidecars say `audio: ok | partial | none` (+ `audioSource`). HOME and the REC pill say "no sound" only when the sound capture failed. `recorder_audio=0` in `irdash.ini` switches only the sound off. Players: the Vault and Debrief players keep his volume; the Workbench has SOUND (key U), off until pressed once.

## Whole sessions (S1)

One recording is one iRacing session. It starts at the first in-car moment and ends when the sim closes or the session changes. Several stints (in and out of the car) go into one file: the gaps are simply skipped. At the end the pieces are joined without re-encoding into `vault\sessions\<date-time>-<track>.mp4` with a `.json` beside it that names the track, car and session type, how long it is, and the laps (with the seconds into the file where each starts) and incidents.

- **Kept by rules.** The last 3 unsaved sessions stay, and unsaved sessions are kept inside 30 GB. An incident (his incident count rising) also cuts a ~30 s clip into `vault\clips\inc-*`. When there is less than 50 GB free the oldest unsaved sessions go first, then old incident clips of sessions that were not kept, then the older unstarred lap clips; if it is still short, recording pauses and says so.
- **SAVE.** The SAVE key (deck `recsave`), the Session Review's SAVE button and the phone's SAVE button keep a session forever: the file moves to `vault\saved\` and is never deleted. Saved sessions are kept inside 150 GB; a SAVE that would pass that is refused and says SAVED FULL. Saving while a session is running or finishing records the wish and moves the file when the finish is complete. An empty SAVE always targets the newest session, including one that is finishing.
- **Disk floor.** Every recorder output (lap clips, incident clips and whole sessions) checks the same 50 GB free-space floor. A cut or save is skipped if it would start with less than that free.
- **After a crash.** If irdash finds leftover pieces from an earlier run (a crash or a deploy), it joins them into one "recovered" session before it clears the scratch folder.
- **Routes.** `GET /api/rec/sessions` lists the sessions (id, track, car, type, start, length, size, saved, laps, incidents); `POST /api/rec/save?id=` (no id = the open session or the newest); `GET /api/rec/session-video?id=` is the mp4 (Range works). `/vault/recorder` also carries the open session's id, length, size, SAVE wish, the recent and saved totals, the free space and the encoder.

## For the next card (video player, C-08)

Where the code is: `dash\src\recorder.cs` (all of it), a few lines in `dash\src\vault.cs` (routes, KEEP, budget) and `dash\src\irdash.cs` (Init/Start). Test: `tests\recorder-test.ps1`.

- **Files.** Rolling pieces: `vault\rec\rec-<UTC start>.ts` (about 10 s each, named by the exact wall-clock start, `yyyyMMdd-HHmmss-fff`; not counted in the Vault's space). Clips: `vault\clips\rec-<UTC lap start>-l<lap>.mp4` + `.json` beside it.
- **Routes.** `GET /vault/video?id=<id>` is the mp4, with `Range` (a `<video>` can seek), 404 when there is none. `<id>` is a clip id (`rec-...`) or a Vault lap id (`l<session>-<lap>`: found by car, track, lap number and the time it was driven). `GET /vault/videoinfo?id=<id>` is the sidecar. `GET /vault/recorder` is the status (running, encoder, black, note, segments, clips).
- **The picture is upright with no filter.** The race screens are rotated 180 degrees in Windows and the capture comes out upside down, so every clip carries the mp4 rotation flag (`rotation:180` in the sidecar). Browsers and ffmpeg honour it. Anything that reads the frames itself (a canvas, a thumbnail) gets them upright from a browser or from ffmpeg without `-noautorotate`.
- **Syncing to telemetry.** The sidecar has `lapStartOffset` and `lapEndOffset`: seconds from the start of the clip's own clock (`videoStart` is where that clock starts, about 0.004). Telemetry time `t` seconds into the lap (the `.ibt`'s own clock from the line) is at `lapStartOffset + t` in the video. The clip's clock is exact to the wall clock the frames were stamped with; the only slack is the screen's own delay (a frame or two from the sim to the screen), which the Vault's `nudge` is for. `clipStartMs`, `lapStartMs`, `lapEndMs` are milliseconds since 1970 (UTC). `truncated:true` means the lap began before the video did (a lap longer than the kept time, or the recorder started mid-lap): the offset is then negative or the end is cut.
- **Not a Vault moment yet.** The recorder does not write `vault\moments.json`. A lap's clip is found by matching, so a lap card can ask `/vault/videoinfo?id=<lap id>` and show the player when it answers 200.
- `CLIP` (the NVIDIA hotkey) and the NVIDIA folder watcher in `vault.cs` are the old draft and are still there; the Paddock recorder replaces them. Do not send Peter to the NVIDIA app.
- **The analysis workbench already plays these clips (W5, `dash\www\v2\workbench-video.js`, `docs\tasks\log\W5-video.md`).** A VIDEO tab and a small video (key V) appear on a lap that has a clip; video time = `lapStartOffset` + the lap's time at the cursor, in both directions, and the reference lap's clip is shown beside it when it has one. A lap can be named three ways for `/vault/video` and `/vault/videoinfo`: a clip id (`rec-...`), a Vault lap id (`l<session>-<lap>`) or **`<telemetry file name>.ibt:<lap>`** (looks the session up by file name, then matches car, track, lap number and when it was driven, like a Vault lap does). The workbench's own `id=` parameter is either of the last two.

## The race log (every car of a session, W6)

`.ibt` files only hold Peter's own car. While a session with other cars runs, irdash (`dash\src\field.cs`, a BelowNormal thread, reads only) logs every car itself and writes one small JSON per session to **`vault\field\f<yyyyMMdd-HHmmss>-<session number>.json`** (about 30 KB for 20 cars x 6 laps; written at every leader lap and at the end, so a crash loses at most a lap; the Vault's budget does not touch these files).

- **What is sampled.** Every 0.1 s of sim time (10 Hz, whatever the frame rate) the `CarIdx` arrays: `LapDistPct`, `Lap`, `LapCompleted`, `Position`, `ClassPosition`, `OnPitRoad`, `TrackSurface`, `LastLapTime`, `BestLapTime`, plus the session text (driver, car, number, team, class and colour, iRating, licence, the results block with incidents). Only sessions with two or more racing cars are logged (a solo test drive costs nothing); a replay is never logged.
- **What is worked out.** Per car: every line crossing (time interpolated by distance between two samples; a crossing counts when the sim's lap counter moved with it, so formation laps do not), lap times (the sim's own `LastLapTime` when it is there, else the difference of two crossings; each lap says which), the time every car crosses the start and end of every corner of the track's corner list (the one the workbench holds), pit stops (in lap, out lap, time on pit road, time in the stall; a flicker under 3 s is no stop), off-track excursions (flicker inside a second is one), retirement, and on-track passes between cars of one class while both are racing on track (a place changed in the pits or by lapping is not a pass). After the chequered flag a car's cool-down laps are not logged.
- **Liveries (P4).** Each car in the file also has `path` (iRacing's `CarPath`, which picks the silhouette) and `dz` (`CarDesignStr` lower-cased: `pattern,colour1,colour2,colour3`). `dash\www\v2\cars.js` draws seven plain silhouettes (GT3, GT4, prototype, GTP, open-wheel, stock car, touring car), body = colour 1, a flank stripe and the wing = colour 2, an accent line = colour 3. A log from before P4, a session text without a design, and iRacing's unset placeholder (`1,FF0000,00FF00,0000FF`) all paint the class colour with no stripes.
- **Precision.** In the pretend race of `tests\race-field-test.ps1` a lap end is within 1 ms of the truth and a corner crossing within 1 ms; the RACE page states **+-0.02 s per lap** (10 Hz, interpolated) because other cars come from the sim's own feed, and the pit lane time and the lap 1 start are good to the sample step, 0.1 s. Corner comparisons are medians of the clean laps (not lap 1, not a pit lap, not an off-track lap, within 107 % of the car's median).
- **Routes.** `GET /analysis/race?id=<field id | telemetry file name | Vault lap id>` returns the log plus the summary (final order, laps, total and gaps, best laps with where each came from, gap to the class leader and position by lap, per-corner medians of every car, passes); a telemetry file or a Vault lap finds the log that ran inside its time window (404 when none). `&x=1` adds the raw corner times. `GET /field/status` has the counters and the sampler's own CPU. 503 while he is driving.
- **The page.** The RACE tab of the workbench (`dash\www\v2\workbench-race.js`) exists only when the session has a log: timing tower, race trace, positions by lap, Peter against the car ahead and the leader (time through each corner), his passes. `workbench.html?id=<file or lap>&lap=<n>&page=race`; `&race=<field id>` names the log directly. **Buttons (W6b):** the Session Review shows RACE next to ANALYSIS and a Vault moment's detail page shows RACE first among its buttons, both only when `GET /analysis/race?id=...` answers 200 (no log, no button); each opens the workbench on the RACE page over the page it came from, with a back bar.

## The career (one car at one track over every session, W6c)

The workbench's CAREER tab (`dash\www\v2\workbench-career.js`, data from `dash\src\career.cs`, `GET /analysis/career?id=<telemetry file name | Vault lap id>` or `?car=&track=&config=`) shows the open lap's car + track + layout over **every** session: the personal best by session (gold = a new PB, a purple ring = the PB now), every clean lap of every session with the average of the best 5 and its spread, every corner against every session (best or median time of the clean laps, purple = the best ever), the first sessions against the latest per corner, the theoretical best (the best iRacing sector ever, joined) against the PB, the races (only when a log in `vault\field` is his car at this track), totals (sessions, laps, clean laps, time driven). Clicking a session opens its best lap; clicking a race opens that session's RACE page. The tab exists only with 2 or more sessions. Key 9 opens it, B switches BEST / MEDIAN.

- **Which files.** Every `.ibt` in iRacing's telemetry folder and every copy under `vault\telemetry` (the original and the Vault's copy of the same session, same start time and size, count once; a copy whose original is gone counts and can be opened by its own name). OneDrive online-only files are skipped (no download). A file written in the last 2 minutes is skipped unless it is the one asked for.
- **Which laps (official times only).** A lap counts when iRacing counted it: the one rule of the next section (L-1), the same in the Session Review, SPLITS, the Vault, the delta bar's PB reference and the Sessions page. Times are iRacing's float32 figure, shown to the 1/1000.
- **Cache.** What one file gives (its counted laps, their sector times and their seconds through every corner of the corner list) is kept in memory and in `vault\career\c<version>-<hash>.json` (a cache of an older format is deleted by the next run), keyed by the file's start time + size and a signature of the corner list. A call after the first reads no `.ibt`. The first call answers `{"building":true,"done":n,"of":m}` and works on ONE BelowNormal thread (the page polls every second), stops when he gets in the car and goes on later; 503 "Your career is shown after your session" while he is on track. `vault\career` is not counted in the Vault's budget (about 5 KB a session).
- **Test.** `tests\career-test.ps1` (copies of real Corvette sessions at Road Atlanta; hand checks against the raw `.ibt`; the live endpoint when irdash has the route).

## Which laps count (one rule everywhere, L-1)

iRacing shows a lap time for almost every lap but only a lap it **counted** can be its session best. Paddock counts exactly those laps, in one place (`LapRules.Judge` in `dash\src\analysis.cs`), and its own best lap (`LapBestLapTime`, read at the last record of the lap and 1.5 to 2.5 s after the line) is the evidence:

1. iRacing gave the lap no time (`LapLastLapTime` -1): not counted (INVALID).
2. The lap is quicker than iRacing's best so far (or it had none): if its best **became** this lap, the lap is counted, whatever the off-track record says (practice sessions count laps with long off-tracks); if its best did **not** change, it is not counted (in the AI races an off-track lap is left out).
3. The lap is not quicker than the best so far: the best cannot tell, so any `PlayerTrackSurface` 0 record in the lap leaves it out (flag OFF), none counts it.

A file without the best-lap channel has no evidence: the off-track record decides alone. Pit laps and part laps never count. A lap that does not count is still shown (grey, with its flag word OFF or INVALID, openable in the workbench, drawn in the stint charts); it is only never "the best". Where it is used: the Session Review (valid, best, optimal, best-5, previous best, reference), SPLITS and its rolling best (same reply), the career, the analysis reference (`pb` / `session` / optimal and the statistics in `/analysis/lap`), the Vault (`VLap.Valid`: PB, BEST LAP, TOP 10, recent items), the Sessions page's best per session (`Career.Read`) and so the delta bar's PB reference (`IbtLaps.Reference` only picks a counted lap; a saved reference from before the rule that is quicker than the best counted lap is not used and is built again). The live bar adopts a new best only when iRacing's own best equals the lap (`PbRef.CheckPending`), which is the same evidence. Rows made under an older rule carry a smaller `LapRules.Version` (the Vault's `sessions.tsv` column 21, the Sessions cache `career-cache.tsv` column 21, `"rules"` in `dash\refs\*.json`) and are read again in the background. Tests: `tests\session-review-test.ps1` (section 3b: per-lap table from the raw records), `analysis-test`, `vault-test` (section 11), `career-test`, `workbench-test`, `pbdelta-test`.

## Share video and the replay jump (P2: `dash\src\share.cs`, `dash\src\replayjump.cs`; tests `share-test`, `replay-jump-test`)

**Share video.** A kept lap clip gets a copy with a broadcast-style data overlay burned in: gear and speed (km/h), throttle and brake bars, the lap time running, the gap to his reference lap (the workbench's reference: his best OTHER clean lap, so the PB lap compares with the next best; words say which), the reference time, a small track map with the car dot and the part driven in gold, and a plate with his name, number, car and track and a small PADDOCK mark. 1920x1080, 60 fps, h264 (NVENC `p5`, `cq 22`, at most 20 Mbit/s; the processor, libx264 veryfast, when the chip refuses), BT.709, `faststart`.

- **Data and sync.** The numbers are read from the lap's own `.ibt` records (60 Hz), not from the workbench's 1 m grid. The lap's time at a record is its `SessionTime` minus the line crossing the Analysis engine found; the clip's pictures come from `ffprobe` (every packet's own time) and picture `n` shows lap time `pts[n] - lapStartOffset`, so the overlay and the picture are the same instant to the frame. The records before and after the lap (the pre-roll and the second after the line) are read too, so the car keeps moving; the lap time sits at 0:00.000 before the line and freezes at the official time after it. Gap = lap time minus the reference's time at the same distance (`TAt`, the workbench's own gap).
- **Upright without the flag.** The clips store the screen rotated 180 degrees with an mp4 rotation flag. ffmpeg's own autorotate applies it in the filter graph and drops it from the output; the job checks the finished file with `ffprobe` (1920x1080, same number of pictures as the clip, no `rotation` side data) and refuses to keep one that fails.
- **Files.** `vault\clips\share-<clip id>.mp4` + `.json` (the name does not start with `rec-`, so the recorder never reads the sidecar as a clip). The newest 10 are kept; one whose clip was cleaned up goes with it. They are not in the Vault's space budget (about 100 - 200 MB each at the most).
- **Jobs.** One at a time, queued, on a BelowNormal thread, ffmpeg BelowNormal. A job starts only when he is not driving, no START key was pressed in the last 10 minutes, and, when the sim is open at all, he has been out of the car for 2 minutes; if he starts driving while one runs, ffmpeg is stopped, the half file deleted, and the job waits ("You are driving...") and starts again. `/share/make` is refused (409) while he drives.
- **Routes.** `GET /share/make?id=<id>[&ref=pb|session|<file>:<lap>][&force=1]` queues (an id is a Vault lap id, `<file>.ibt:<lap>` or a recorder clip id) and answers the status; `GET /share/status?id=` -> `{state: none|queued|waiting|making|done|error, pct, reason, queue, ready, size, name, encoder, made, seconds}`; `GET /share/video?id=` serves the finished file with Range (404 when it has not been made); `GET /share/open?id=` opens Explorer with the file selected (this PC only; it only ever opens the file the job itself wrote). Test hook only: `&stripe=1` on make adds 77 black / white blocks along the bottom edge (speed x100 17 bits, gear + 1 4, throttle x1000 10, brake x1000 10, (lap time + 20 s) in ms 20, (gap + 30 s) in ms 16, 65535 = none, 18 px blocks from x = 8) and `/share/test/frame?id=&t=` returns the strip drawn for lap time `t` as a picture.
- **Where the buttons are.** The workbench VIDEO page: the chip `MAKE SHARE VIDEO` (progress `MAKING 42 %`, then `SHOW SHARE VIDEO`), `dash\www\v2\workbench-share.js`. The phone's CLIPS screen: a card under the player (Make share video, progress bar, Save share video, Share); on plain http the share sheet is not available, so Share saves the file and says "Saved - share it from Photos". The phone's list now also shows the recorder's own clips (from `/vault/recorder`), which it did not before.

**Replay jump.** `GET /share/jump?id=<file or Vault lap id>&lap=<n>&t=<s into the lap>` (an event) or `&st=<session time>` (a pass of the race log), `&car=<CarIdx|me>`, `&lead=3`, `&dry=1`. The event's session (`SessionNum`, `SessionUniqueID` from the .ibt records, `SubSessionID` from its text) is compared with what the sim has open now; the same `SessionUniqueID` (or the same non-zero `SubSessionID` with that session number on the tape) is the same session, anything else is "iRacing has a different session open". Then exactly three SDK broadcast messages: `ReplaySearchSessionTime(session, (time - 3 s) ms)`, `CamSwitchNum(his car or the passer, the camera that was on)`, `ReplaySetPlaySpeed(1)` (the TV director is switched off first when it was on). Never while he is driving, never `ReplaySetState`. Buttons `SEE IN iRACING` sit on the event rows and the passes; they are hidden unless the dry run says yes (asked every 5 s). To check on the first real use: whether the sim's `SessionUniqueID` stays the same while its replay is open (the code assumes it does) - the dry run's `liveUnique` / `unique` fields show both numbers.
